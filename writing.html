Writing advice

How to write

Two different jobs, with different rules.

The essays you submit

The main thing you are judged on. Up to five.

Length
1,000–1,500 words — never under 500
Rate
One a week, so each gets real effort
Aim
Argument-driven, one clear thesis, a specific audience
Topics
From the heat map below

The daily posts

The public track record the form asks about.

Length
500 words is enough
Rate
Every day
Where
LessWrong counts most — engagement there is scored
Aim
Fresh, not a polished backlog. Quantity beats polish.

Karma is LessWrong's upvote score. Ben Pace's rough test for whether you would get in: a couple of comments at 50+, or a post or two at 100+.

What to write about

We read every post from the last two cohorts, and every post Inkhaven picked out as worth reading. These are the most seminal topics. The higher the percentage, the more of the community's writing a topic accounts for. Green ones the community rates especially highly, clearly enough to trust; blue ones it rates highly too, but not clearly enough to rule out luck. The rest show no advantage either way.

The five essays that you will submit should be from these categories. Note, however, that a rare category will not necessarily make you stand out. Rather you should match the collective interests of the community and write on topics that are popular. Your differentiation lies in writing novel and compelling essays within each category.
