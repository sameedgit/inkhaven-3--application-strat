Relevant reading

Four things that bear directly on the application. The summaries below already carry most of what these posts have to offer, so you can rely on them alone and only open the originals if you want the full thing in the author's voice.

For Your Consideration: Inkhaven 2

Vishal Prasad — resident, then organiser
  • Do a 30-day blogging sprint before the window closes — 30 posts, 500+ words each.
  • Produce at least one discourse-generating effort-post. He credits his own "spicy effort-post" for getting in.
  • For nonfiction, write clear, persuasive, argument-driven prose. No schizo-posting, no lifestyle blogging.
  • If cost is the barrier, email them — many of cohort 1 were subsidised.

The Inkhaven Residency

Ben Pace — the original announcement
  • Ben Pace gives a rough test for whether you would get in. On LessWrong, readers upvote posts and comments; the running score is called karma. If you have written a couple of comments that scored 50+, or a post or two that scored 100+, he takes that as a good sign you can do this.
  • It prints the application question in full, and Ben Pace answers applicants directly in the comments — so you can see what they do and don't care about.

The Hacker News thread

Oliver Habryka, in the comments
  • Habryka explains they read applications as they arrive, not all at the end. So early applicants are judged before the organisers know how strong the field will be — applying early can mean an easier bar.
  • So we should get the application in by the last week of September at the latest.

So you want to go to next year's Inkhaven

Amanda Luce — how to run a daily sprint
  • Write fresh, simple posts — do not spend the month polishing a backlog.
  • Keep a sustainable daily rhythm: draft early, get feedback, publish before dinner.
  • Cap research-heavy effort-posts at about one a week. More is not sustainable.
